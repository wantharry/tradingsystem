# Strategy 5: Post-Event Momentum Drift

**Family:** Event | **Best Regime:** Event  
**Difficulty:** Intermediate  
**Expected Win Rate:** ~50-60% | **Target R:R:** 2:1

---

## What Is This Strategy? (Plain English)

When a stock gaps up 3% on earnings or news, two things can happen:
1. **The gap fills** — sellers rush in, price falls back (fade the gap)
2. **The gap holds** — buyers stay strong, price drifts higher for days (ride the drift)

This strategy **only trades scenario 2** — when the initial reaction is so strong that the stock *holds its gain* all day. This signals genuine, sustained demand from institutions who need to keep buying.

The "drift" effect is well-documented: stocks that gap up on positive earnings continue drifting up for 2-10 more days as more investors pile in after reading the news.

---

## Entry Rules

1. **Large gap:** Today's open is ≥ 2% higher (or lower) than yesterday's close
2. **Volume spike:** Today's volume ≥ 2× the 20-day average (institutions are active)
3. **Gap holds:** Close is within 25% of the gap size from open (didn't give back the gain)
   - Example: Gapped from $100 to $105 (+5%). If it closes above $103.75, gap is holding
4. **Enter:** Next day market open (join the drift)

---

## Exit Rules

**Stop:** 1.5× ATR below entry  
**Target:** 2× risk distance above entry  
**Time Stop:** 15 days maximum (drift effect fades after 2 weeks)

---

## Why This Strategy Has Edge

Post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD) is one of the most documented market anomalies in academic finance. Discovered by Ball & Brown in 1968 and replicated dozens of times. The effect exists because:

1. Institutional investors can't buy huge positions in a single day
2. They "accumulate" over days and weeks
3. This steady buying pressure pushes prices higher

The volume spike filter ensures we're only trading events with real institutional interest, not retail-driven moves that often reverse.
